# Business strategy and project delivery

## Purpose

Help make commercial decisions from customer and operating evidence. Label
proposals, targets, forecasts, competitor claims, and assumptions distinctly.
This module is a planning framework, not legal, tax, employment, or investment
advice.

## Customer and market reasoning

Define the customer segment, job/problem, current alternatives, purchase
authority, and evidence of willingness to pay. Separate interview statements,
observed behaviour, paid pilots, repeat purchase, and market estimates; they
have different evidential strength. A large theoretical market does not prove
reachable demand.

Use competitor comparisons with dates and comparable scope. Do not infer
competitor pricing, market share, or capabilities from unverified claims.
Include a "do nothing" alternative and identify what evidence could falsify the
value proposition.

## Unit economics

Contribution per unit is revenue per unit minus variable cost per unit, using
consistent treatment of discounts, returns, fulfilment, payment fees, and
support. Break-even volume is fixed cost divided by positive contribution per
unit. If contribution is zero or negative, that formula does not yield a
meaningful positive break-even target.

Distinguish gross margin from markup:

- gross margin = (selling price − cost of goods sold) / selling price
- markup = (selling price − cost of goods sold) / cost of goods sold

State the denominator and whether operating expenses, tax, financing, and
working capital are included. A spreadsheet output is only as reliable as its
inputs.

## Strategy and delivery

Map goals to a small number of choices: target customer, differentiated value,
capabilities, channels, and activities to stop. SWOT/PEST-style frameworks are
prompts for evidence, not proof or predictions.

A delivery plan should include outcomes, scope, milestones, dependencies,
capacity, owner proposals, risks, acceptance criteria, budget assumptions,
and review gates. Stage irreversible commitments. Track leading indicators
without treating them as guaranteed outcomes. Include customer, operational,
people, cash-flow, regulatory, and reputation risks.

## Verification sources

For Australian obligations, consult current ATO, ASIC, ACCC, business.gov.au
and relevant state/territory authorities; exact obligations depend on
structure, activity, and jurisdiction. Use dated customer research and
reconciled internal accounts for commercial conclusions. These sources are
verification targets, not proof that a particular business has complied.
