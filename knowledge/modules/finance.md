# Finance and investment analysis foundations

## Purpose

Create traceable, dated analysis—not a forecast disguised as fact or
personalized financial advice. Separate facts, assumptions, arithmetic,
interpretation, scenarios, risks, and invalidation conditions.

## Financial statements

- **Income statement:** revenue and expenses over a period; profit is not cash
  flow.
- **Balance sheet:** assets, liabilities, and equity at a date; check whether
  balances are current/non-current and whether accounting estimates matter.
- **Cash-flow statement:** operating, investing, and financing cash movements.
  Reconcile cash generation with capital expenditure, working capital, debt,
  and shareholder distributions.

Use the same reporting period, currency, units, and accounting basis before
comparing. Normalize only with a documented method. Distinguish reported
figures, company guidance, analyst estimates, and the assistant's assumptions.

## Returns, valuation, and uncertainty

Simple return over a period is \((P_1-P_0+D)/P_0\), where \(P_0\) and \(P_1\)
are comparable prices and \(D\) is cash distribution per unit during that
period. State whether prices are adjusted for distributions and corporate
actions, the currency, and the dates. Annualizing a short or irregular period
can mislead.

A discounted cash-flow model estimates value as the present value of future
cash flows plus terminal value, using a discount rate consistent with the cash
flow definition and currency. It is highly sensitive to growth, margins,
reinvestment, discount rate, and terminal assumptions. Show scenarios and
sensitivity; do not imply that a precise output is a precise truth.

Multiples compare market value with a defined accounting or operating metric.
Check enterprise versus equity value, debt/cash treatment, share count,
period, growth, cyclicality, and peer comparability. A low multiple alone does
not establish undervaluation.

## Portfolio and risk controls

Check position units, prices, currency conversion date, cash, liabilities,
concentration, sector exposure, liquidity, tax treatment, and coverage
completeness. Unknown or stale prices remain unavailable, not zero. Correlation
describes co-movement in a sample; it is not causation or a guarantee of
diversification under stress. Survivorship and look-ahead bias can distort
historical comparisons.

Every thesis should name the evidence that supports it, strongest counter-case,
key assumptions, downside paths, catalysts (if verified), and conditions that
would invalidate it. Separate historical facts from scenarios. Do not claim
live prices without dated market data.

## Verification sources

Prefer dated issuer/ASX filings and reports for company claims; RBA and ABS for
Australian macro data; ASIC MoneySmart for general investor education; and
documented market data for prices. Record source date, retrieval date,
currency, units, and coverage gaps. This foundation is not a personal
suitability assessment or financial advice.
