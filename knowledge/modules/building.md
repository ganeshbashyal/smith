# Australian building science and evidence boundaries

## Purpose

Explain building performance mechanisms and organize evidence for a project.
This module does not determine NCC compliance, certify a product, or replace a
building surveyor, engineer, fire-safety practitioner, or other qualified
professional.

## Performance levels

Keep four levels distinct:

1. **Material property** — a measured property of a material under a defined
   test.
2. **Product performance** — the result for a defined product, specimen,
   method, thickness, fixing, and test condition.
3. **Assembly performance** — the combined system including framing, cavities,
   linings, joints, penetrations, restraints, and boundary conditions.
4. **Installed-building performance** — the as-built result including
   workmanship, interfaces, climate, maintenance, and operation.

Evidence at one level does not automatically prove the next. A manufacturer's
technical data sheet is useful product evidence but is not independent
certification or regulatory approval.

## Heat, air, moisture, and acoustics

Heat moves by conduction, convection, and radiation. A higher R-value indicates
greater resistance to heat flow for the stated component and method; it does
not alone establish whole-wall performance or energy savings. Thermal bridges,
air leakage, glazing, shading, ventilation, moisture, and installation affect
the whole assembly.

Moisture can move as liquid water, water vapour by diffusion, and humid air
through leakage. Condensation risk depends on temperature profiles, indoor
humidity, air movement, climate, materials, and construction details. Vapour
control is climate- and assembly-specific; generic layering advice can create
trapped moisture.

Acoustic ratings describe defined test outcomes and are not interchangeable
with thermal R-values. Rw and related ratings apply to a specified tested
element; flanking paths, junctions, penetrations, installation, and background
noise influence field performance. Ask which metric, test method, specimen,
and field condition is meant.

## Compliance evidence checklist

Before any code conclusion, establish jurisdiction; building class and use;
approval/design date; applicable NCC edition and amendments; relevant
provisions; referenced standard edition; approved design pathway; and the
exact product/system installation. Distinguish Deemed-to-Satisfy provisions
from Performance Solution evidence and approval. Do not invent clause numbers,
fire ratings, acoustic results, certification, or approval.

## Practical comparison

Compare products only with declared thickness, density, framing, fixing,
orientation, climate zone, and test method. Record missing documents and
conflicting claims. An exact SKU and current revision matter. If no reliable
match exists, report that rather than substituting a similar product.

## Verification sources

For a project, retrieve the applicable NCC/ABCB materials, state or territory
regulator guidance, project approval documents, exact manufacturer TDS/SDS and
test reports, and qualified practitioner assessment. YourHome and government
energy information can help with general building-science context but do not
establish project compliance. Confirm current editions and terms before use.
